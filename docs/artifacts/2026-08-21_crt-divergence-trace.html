Two predictions, written before the run

The causes are not annotations invented for this drawing — each divergent bar carries a member of the existing CATEGORY_PRECEDENCE taxonomy, which makes the diagram a live test of it. Both predictions were sealed in the plan before the extractor ran.

Prediction 1 · EXPANSION → C-GEOMETRY · confirmed, after a correction

Confirmed — but the first run said D-UNKNOWN, and the cause was mine.

The classifier’s A- and C- branches are declared allowlists, not inference: C-GEOMETRY fires only for pairs present in known_geometry_divergent_pairs. The first execution passed an empty MismatchContext, collapsing almost everything to the fallthrough — which would have read as a taxonomy gap when it was an under-configured run. Wiring in crt_parity_report’s canonical sets resolved all sixteen EXPANSION bars to C-GEOMETRY.

Prediction 2 · SHADOW_PENDING → a taxonomy gap · confirmed

SHADOW_PENDING classifies as INSUFFICIENT — “not enough data to say” — when the truth is a deductive certainty.

STRUCTURALLY_UNREACHABLE_STATES holds exactly three members: EXECUTION, RESOLUTION, EXPIRED. The YAML’s three when: {} states are SHADOW_PENDING, EXPIRED, RESOLUTION. SHADOW_PENDING is in one list and not the other. Missing the structural branch, it falls to the power gate — engine occupancy 6, below MIN_CELL_N=15 — and is labelled under-powered.

This is a mislabel of kind, not degree, and it is context-independent: the structural check runs first and reads only a module constant, so no amount of context changes it. The classifier’s own precedence comment explains why that ordering exists — structural unreachability is “a deductive fact from source, Certain even at low n”. SHADOW_PENDING qualifies and was omitted.

D-UNKNOWN on DISPLACEMENT and RETEST is not a third gap. It is the classifier’s explicit fallthrough — “investigated, never left silently unlabeled” — and RETEST in particular is a deliberate exclusion from the geometry set, recorded in RETEST_CASCADE_NOTE because its n=17 sits at the power floor. A recorded choice, not an absence.

Two hazards this trace surfaced

Bar counts: one frame, two derivations

Not a conflict once written down — but leaving it implicit is how a holdout gets cut against the wrong denominator.

47,275 raw corpus
− 78 warmup → 47,197 resolver frame
− 40 horizon → 47,157 scan frame

Bar indices: two spaces, not one

An events.jsonl candle_index is the engine’s own counter, not a raw CSV row. Conflating them silently slices the wrong bars — which this extractor did on its first run, producing a plausible-looking and entirely wrong trace.

measured offset +62 (6,991 events)
docstring asserts +74 — stale

Both hazards are now recorded in the fixture itself rather than in prose, and the extractor takes --window-space explicitly so the ambiguity cannot be re-entered by accident.
