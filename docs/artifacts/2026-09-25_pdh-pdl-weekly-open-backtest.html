PDH–PDL Weekly-Open Backtest
XAUUSD · M15 · 22 May 2024 – 21 May 2026 · 1 oz · measured broker costs

PDH / PDL + weekly open: 2-year gold backtest

This is a mechanical test of the "PDH & PDL range, weekly open as key level" map on gold. Every number below comes from files in our repository, and each trade is listed with a timestamp so you can check it on your own chart.

Scope — read this first (corrected 26 Sep 2026). Every result on this page tests a simplified mechanical version of pieces of Sujan's method. It does not test his complete method, and none of these rules has been confirmed by Sujan as his own. His full chain includes several steps we never measured: the HTF zone, the 5m/15m displacement, the retest, his 5-minute entry, his stop and target rules, the volume signal and his bias indicator. Section 25 lists them. So the results below say "this simplified rule has no edge". They do not say "Sujan's method has no edge".

Result in one paragraph

Sweep branch (price pokes beyond PDH/PDL and closes back inside, then reverse): over 424 trades the average result before costs is about zero (+0.01R), and no weekly-open filter changes that. Break + accept branch (two closes beyond PDH/PDL, then continue) is the only part that beats a coin-flip direction on the same trades. But it makes its money on breaks above PDH (+₹51,851) and loses on breaks below PDL (−₹46,416). Gold rose about 88% in this period, and just holding gold during the same trade windows earned ₹4,000–4,650 per month, against ₹230–1,170 for the strategy.

This tests a simplified version of the map, set out in section 2. It does not test the full discretionary process. Section 7 lists what is missing, and your corrections there matter most.

1 · The levels, as we drew them

PDH (upper) and PDL (lower): previous day's high and low Weekly open (red line)
  • Day = broker server day (MT5, ICMarkets). The daily candle closes at 17:00 New York time, the same cut as the usual daily candle for gold. PDH and PDL come only from the finished previous day, so no future data is used.
  • Weekly open = opening price of the first 15-minute bar of the week (Monday 01:00 broker time = Sunday evening New York).
  • Checked on every one of 47,183 bars against a separate calculation: 0 mismatches for PDH, PDL and weekly open.

2 · The trade rules (fixed before the run)

Sweep → reversal

  • Sweep of PDH: a 15-min bar trades above PDH and closes back below it, so SHORT.
  • Sweep of PDL: the mirror image, so LONG.
  • Entry (y): open of the next bar.
  • Stop (x): the sweep bar's extreme ± $0.50.

Break → accept → continuation

  • Break of PDH: two 15-min closes in a row above PDH (the first cross of the day), so LONG.
  • Break of PDL: the mirror image, so SHORT.
  • Entry (y): open of the next bar.
  • Stop (x): the far extreme of the two accept bars ± $0.50.
  • Target: either 2× the risk (2R), or, for sweeps only, the other side of the previous day's range.
  • Weekly-open location, three readings, all reported:
    • none: no filter.
    • premium/discount: short only above the weekly open, long only below it.
    • trend side: long only above the weekly open, short only below it.
  • Position rules: each level is used once per day, and only one position is open at a time.
  • Fills: if a bar touches both stop and target, we count the stop. If price gaps through the stop, the fill is at the gap price.

7 · What this test does NOT include (please correct us)

Your XAU reference map has steps that we reduced to simple rules or left out. If any of these is what makes the method work, tell us the exact rule and we will test it.

  • HTF resistance zone (for example 4399–4404): not used. We used only PDH/PDL. Which timeframe and which candle define that zone?
  • "Bearish reaction → 5m/15m displacement → retest" after the sweep: not required. We entered on the next bar after the sweep. How big must the displacement be, and where exactly is the retest entry?
  • "Break → accept": we defined accept as two 15-min closes beyond the level. Is acceptance measured on 15m, 1h or 4h, and how many closes?
  • "Vol 100 – sell (distribution)": not used. Which volume indicator and threshold?
  • Weekly open "location important": we tested both readings (premium/discount and trend side). Which one do you use?
  • Targets: we used 2R or the opposite side of the range. The map shows 4045 → 3942. How is the target chosen?
  • Pairs: gold only. EURAUD, EURNZD, NZDCHF, GBPAUD, NZDJPY and AUDUSD are not tested yet.

11 · How every rupee figure is calculated

Profit in $ per oz = direction × (exit price − entry price) direction: long +1, short −1 − entry costs $0.175 = half spread 0.045 + commission 0.040 + slippage 0.090 − exit costs $0.085 = half spread 0.045 + commission 0.040 + overnight fee × nights held (long −0.609 / short +0.426 per night; a Wednesday night counts 3) Stop exits are also filled $0.09 worse than the stop price (slippage), inside the exit price. Rupees = profit in $ × 1 oz × ₹84 per $

13 · What was asked, and the rules we used

Sujan (25 Sep, 21:02–21:05): "Put 1 hr candle close after sweep, not 15 min … we are trading daily range acting as a weekly open key level, so 1 hr confirmation required … I think we get more win rate for 1 hr." Also: "15 min data is good 3% per month profitability … with funded account per month 3 lakhs."

  • 1-hour candles are built from the same 15-minute data, on the clock hour. For example, "04:00" covers 04:00–04:59 broker time. The hour is only used once it has closed, and the entry is the open of the next hour.
  • 1H sweep (reading 1): a 1-hour candle trades beyond PDH/PDL and closes back inside. The stop is that hour's wick ± $0.50.
  • 15m sweep → 1H close (reading 2): the 15-minute sweep as before, then we wait for that hour to close back inside the level. If it closes outside, there is no trade that day. The stop is the extreme from the sweep candle to the end of the hour ± $0.50.
  • 1H break: two 1-hour closes in a row beyond PDH/PDL (the first cross of the day). The stop is the far extreme of the two hours ± $0.50. Both readings use this same break rule, so their break rows are identical.
  • Unchanged:
    • exits checked every 15 minutes
    • 2R target (and the day-range target for sweeps)
    • the three weekly-open readings
    • once per level per day, one position at a time
    • 1 oz, measured costs
  • Our prediction, written before the run: the win rate may rise a little, but stops get wider, so the result per unit of risk (R) stays about the same. Only the coin-flip control and holding gold decide whether it is better.

What the 1-hour close changed
  • Win rate: sweeps with no filter rose by 1.6–2.4 points (34.4% → 36.0% / 36.8%), and by up to 4.3 points with the premium/discount reading. With the trend-side reading they fell (39.0% → 31.7% / 35.6%). Breaks did not rise (39.7% → 39.9%; trend side 40.3% → 38.3%). So "more win rate" holds a little for sweeps, and not for breaks.
  • ₹ per month rose a lot: sweep −₹320 → about +₹2,500, break +₹227 → +₹5,316. But the result per unit of risk barely moved (sweep −0.05R → +0.02/+0.03R; break +0.14R → +0.17R), and every 95% range still crosses zero. The rupees rose mainly because the stops roughly doubled (median sweep risk $5.32 → $9.30; break $8.99 → $17.64). Rupees = R × risk, so the same R on a wider stop is more rupees, and also bigger losses.
  • Coin flip: none of the 2R arms on 1H beats it. For the 1H break, random directions on the same entries average +0.08R, so part of the result comes from when the trades are taken (volatile moments), not from the direction.
  • New on 1H: the break branch now makes money in both directions (breaks above PDH +₹94,565, breaks below PDL +₹32,744; at 15m the shorts lost −₹46,416). It also beats holding gold over the same windows (+₹5,316 vs +₹2,404 a month).
  • One day-range-target arm beats the coin flip: 15m→1H · sweep · trend side (83 trades, +0.23R, +₹1,152/month). It is 1 of 15 distinct new arms, and about 1 in 20 would pass by chance alone, so treat it as a lead, not a result.
  • Overlap: the two sweep readings share 293 of their trades (353 and 307 in total), so they are mostly the same trades.

21 · The mechanical 1-hour CRT rule over 2 years (a simplified version, not his full method)

Rules, fixed before the run:

  • The engine: the repository's existing 3-candle CRT construct (ParentCRTTrack, unchanged), fed closed 1-hour candles.
  • C2 must sweep C1's high or low and close strictly inside C1.
  • Entry at the C3 open. Target at C1's far side.
  • Stops: S1 = C2's wick ± $0.50; S2 = the midpoint between entry and C2's wick, a tight stop like his.
  • Location sets:
    • L0 = no gate
    • L1 = C2's wick swept PDH/PDL and closed back
    • L2 = L1 plus the weekly open or day open
  • Our prediction: close to zero R, with a possibly high win rate because the target is near.

What this shows
  • Location matters, and Sujan is right about that. Without it the rule loses in every block (−₹2,849/month, 45.6% wins). With a PDH/PDL location the win rate is 61.8%, and with the weekly/day-open set it is 56.9%.
  • A high win rate is still not profit. The target (C1's far side) is usually closer than the stop (C2's wick), so the average loss is 1.5–1.8× the average win. With L1, the win rate needed just to break even is 1,046 ÷ (589 + 1,046) = 64%, against 61.8% achieved. With L2 it is 59.5% needed against 56.9%. Both miss by about 2–3 points.
  • Only L2 with the wide stop beats the coin flip, but its R range still crosses zero and its rupee result is negative. It is a lead, not an edge.
  • A tight stop like his cuts the win rate to 31–40%, because price often dips back through the middle before reaching the target. On 25 Sep his stop survived by $1.11.
  • Fresh 2026 data points the same way (L1 57.7% wins, +₹773/month), but with only 26 trades it is too few to judge.
  • Entries across the daily close barely matter here: 2 of 136 trades for L1, 5 of 411 for L2.
